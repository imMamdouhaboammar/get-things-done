# Consulting Frameworks — 12 Battle-Tested Advisory Lenses

> Use this reference at Step 2 (Read Situation) and Step 3 (Choose Mode). Pick the **single most load-bearing framework** for the situation. Do not dump multiple frameworks on the user — that generates cognitive clutter and recreates analysis paralysis.

---

## The 12 Frameworks at a Glance

| Framework | Core Question | Ideal Application |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Real vs. Stated Problem** | What is the unspoken root cause? | Generic questions, sudden urgency, recurring symptoms |
| **2. The Reversibility Test** | How easily can this be undone? | High-stakes anxiety, build vs buy, commitment fears |
| **3. The 10/10/10 Horizon** | How will this look in 10min / 10mo / 10yr? | Emotionally charged decisions, temporary embarrassment |
| **4. Regret Minimization** | At age 80, which path will haunt you? | Major life pivots, founder leaps, career crossroads |
| **5. The Energy & Vitality Audit** | Which option gives energy vs draining it? | Ambiguous career options, co-founder chemistry |
| **6. Constraint Reframe** | Is this constraint Hard, Soft, or Assumed? | "I have no choice" statements, resource bottlenecks |
| **7. The Pre-Mortem** | It's 12 months later and this failed — why? | High-optimism plans, major launches, blindspot audits |
| **8. Second-Order Consequences** | And what does THAT lead to? | Short-term fixes, tech debt, pricing changes |
| **9. Asymmetric Risk/Reward** | Is downside bounded and upside uncapped? | Side projects, experiments, bold strategic bets |
| **10. Load-Bearing Assumption** | Which single belief MUST be true for this to work? | Unproven business models, unvalidated features |
| **11. Opportunity Cost Triangulation**| What are you implicitly killing by saying yes? | Overloaded roadmaps, feature requests, consulting gigs |
| **12. Kill-Criteria & Re-evaluation** | What exact metric triggers an immediate exit? | Speculative ventures, turnaround projects, pivots |

## Deep Framework Specifications

### 1. Real vs. Stated Problem
People frequently present the symptom because the root cause carries emotional friction or identity threat.
- **Diagnostic Method:** Apply the "Five Whys of Intent" or ask: *"If we solved this exact problem tomorrow, what would you still be worried about?"*
- **Application:** When an engineer asks *"How do I rewrite our monolith in Rust?"*, the real problem is usually *"Our deployments are breaking and the team has lost confidence in our release cycle."*

### 2. The Reversibility Test (Type 1 vs. Type 2 Decisions)
- **Type 1 (One-Way Door / Irreversible):** Heavy capital expenditure, signing long-term leases, selling majority equity, public brand commitments. **Rule:** Slow down, widen the option space, conduct pre-mortems.
- **Type 2 (Two-Way Door / Reversible):** Pricing tier experiments, temporary contractor hires, feature MVPs, internal tooling choices. **Rule:** Decide fast (<24h), execute cleanly, treat outcome as data.

### 3. The 10/10/10 Horizon
Separates acute emotional discomfort from durable long-term value.
- **10 Minutes:** Acute fear, social awkwardness, adrenaline, dread. (Usually painful but transient).
- **10 Months:** Operational reality, habit formation, compound results.
- **10 Years:** Fundamental life trajectory, character alignment, legacy.
- **Application:** Having a difficult conversation with a underperforming co-founder is 10/10 painful for 10 minutes, but 10/10 relief and growth across 10 months and 10 years.

### 4. Regret Minimization Framework
- **Diagnostic Question:** *"When you are 80 years old looking back across your life, which choice will you regret not attempting?"*
- **Boundary:** Do not apply to minor tactical problems. Reserved for fundamental life vectors: starting a company, moving across countries, ending an unviable partnership.

### 5. The Energy & Vitality Audit
Logic often rationalizes choices that drain vitality.
- **The Tuesday Test:** *"Imagine a typical Tuesday 12 months from now under Option A. Walk me through your morning, afternoon, and evening. Now do the same for Option B. Which Tuesday gives you energy?"*
- **Insight:** Sustainable execution requires intrinsic energy. If an option looks great on paper but drains your soul on a Tuesday afternoon, it will fail.

### 6. The Constraint Reframe
Categorize every cited blocker into one of three buckets:
1. **Hard Constraint:** Laws of physics, strict statutory regulations, mathematical realities. (Must be designed around).
2. **Soft Constraint:** Requires money, time, negotiation, or trade-offs to move. (Can be solved with resources).
3. **Assumed Constraint:** A belief inherited from convention or past failure that has never been tested in this context. (Must be challenged immediately).

### 7. The Pre-Mortem Failure Analysis
- **Method:** *"Imagine we are sitting here exactly one year from today, and this plan has suffered a catastrophic failure. Write the headline and list the top 3 structural causes that broke it."*
- **Benefit:** Removes optimism bias and transforms defensive planning into proactive risk immunization.

### 8. Second- and Third-Order Consequences Check
- **First Order:** Immediate intended effect (e.g., cut prices by 50% to win market share).
- **Second Order:** Competitor reaction and customer perception (e.g., competitors match price; customers perceive low quality).
- **Third Order:** Margin destruction and inability to fund R&D (e.g., business becomes unprofitable and unviable).

### 9. Asymmetric Risk/Reward Ratio
- **Favorable Asymmetry:** Maximum loss is fixed and tolerable (e.g., \$5K and 2 weeks of time), while potential upside is multi-fold (e.g., new recurring revenue stream). **Action: Bias to execution.**
- **Unfavorable Asymmetry:** Small upside (e.g., saving \$200/mo on infrastructure) with catastrophic downside (e.g., 48-hour global outage and data loss). **Action: Reject immediately.**

### 10. Load-Bearing Assumption Falsification
Every complex plan rests on 1–2 foundational assumptions.
- **Method:** Identify the single assumption that, if false, invalidates the entire project. Design the cheapest possible test to falsify this assumption before writing code or spending capital.

### 11. Opportunity Cost Triangulation
- **Method:** When deciding to take on a new project or client, explicitly name what gets deprioritized or eliminated.
- **Rule:** Saying "yes" to a non-core opportunity is an implicit "no" to your core mission. Make the implicit sacrifice explicit.

### 12. Kill-Criteria & Re-evaluation Triggers
- **Method:** Before committing to a speculative path, establish non-negotiable exit criteria.
- **Example:** *"If we do not acquire 25 paying users with >40% weekly retention within 90 days of launch, we will shut down this feature line rather than pouring more engineering hours into it."*
