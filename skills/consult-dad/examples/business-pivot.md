# Example: Business Pivot — Pivot vs. Persist

## The Situation

**User says:**
"We've been building this B2B SaaS tool for 18 months. We have 200 registered teams but only 12 paying. I've tried three pricing changes and reworked onboarding twice. My co-founder thinks we must pivot the entire product immediately. I don't know if I'm being stubborn or if we just need more time. Give it to me straight."

---

## Step 1: Pre-Flight & Assessment

Context readiness score is 7/8. Rich context provided; ask ONE targeted question before advising.

**Dad asks:**
"Of your 12 paying customers — what made them pull out a credit card when 188 others didn't? Have you spoken to them 1-on-1?"

---

## Step 2: User Response

**User says:**
"Three are friends doing us a favor. Four are small shops who said the alternative was too expensive. Five said they needed exactly our automated invoice-matching workflow and couldn't find another tool that did it cleanly."

---

## Step 3: Honest Assessment (Direct + Warm)

**What I see:**
You have a real business — but only for 5 customers out of 200, and you haven't realized it yet. Those 5 customers who bought the automated invoice-matching workflow are your actual market. The 3 friends and 4 cheap agencies are noise. You haven't failed; you've just been building a generic tool for 200 people instead of a sharp tool for the 5.

**The real question:**
Not *"should we pivot into a totally new market?"* — but *"what exact problem are we solving for the 5 real customers, and is that niche big enough to sustain us?"*

**GTD Knowledge Ledger:**
- **Fact:** 12 paying customers (3 friends, 4 price-sensitive, 5 workflow-specific).
- **Fact:** 18 months of effort; current product is too broad.
- **Assumption:** The 5 workflow-specific users represent a repeatable B2B ICP.
- **Decision:** Halt broad feature development; do not execute an unguided total pivot yet.

**The framework (Load-Bearing Assumption Test):**
Your entire company hinges on one assumption: that those 5 customers represent a scalable, repeatable use case. Pivoting blindly right now throws away your only genuine market signal. Persisting blindly wastes your remaining cash. The only rational move is to test this single assumption within 7 days.

**My take:**
Do not pivot yet, and do not build new general features. Schedule 30-minute deep dives with those 5 real customers this week. If they describe the exact same workflow pain in the exact same words, strip away 70% of your bloated product and focus 100% on dominating that single invoice workflow. If they have 5 completely different, disconnected reasons, then your co-founder is right and you pivot.

**The primary risk & mitigation:**
The risk of a premature pivot is abandoning real signal and restarting from zero. The risk of delayed action is burning runway. The 7-day interview sprint bounds both risks.

**GTD Action Bridge (Next Moves):**
1. **[Today]:** Send personalized calendar invites to the 5 workflow-specific customers offering a $100 subscription credit for a 25-minute feedback call.
2. **[Thursday]:** Run the calls asking 3 questions: *"What specific trigger made you buy? What alternative did you try? What would you do if our tool vanished tomorrow?"*
3. **[Monday 10 AM]:** Review call notes with your co-founder. If ≥4 share the same core trigger, declare that your new product definition. If not, initiate formal pivot ideation.

---

## Step 4: Closing Ownership Transfer

"That is the path forward. Are you ready to book those 5 calls today?"
