# Example: Founder Conflict — Co-Founder Equity & Role Realignment

## The Situation

**User says:**
"My co-founder and I started this company 12 months ago with a 50/50 equity split. Over the last 6 months, he has completely checked out — working maybe 15 hours a week while I work 70. We are about to raise our Seed round and investors are asking about team commitment. I'm furious and resentful, but I'm terrified that bringing this up will blow up the company. What would you do?"

---

## Step 1: Pre-Flight & Assessment

Context readiness score is 8/8. High-stakes interpersonal and business crisis. No basic clarifying question needed — dive into honest situation reading.

---

## Step 2: Honest Assessment (Direct + Warm)

**What I see:**
You are avoiding the conversation because you fear it will destroy the company. In reality, **avoiding the conversation is what will destroy the company.** Cap tables do not fix themselves, and professional Seed investors will spot an uncommitted 50% co-founder in due diligence immediately. A clean split now is painful for 2 weeks; an unresolved dispute 6 months from now during a fundraise is fatal.

**The real question:**
Not *"how do I keep him happy while doing all the work?"* — but *"what is the equitable ownership structure that reflects our actual future commitment to this business?"*

**GTD Knowledge Ledger:**
- **Fact:** 50/50 equity split currently on standard 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff.
- **Fact:** User is working full-time (70h/wk); co-founder is contributing part-time (~15h/wk).
- **Fact:** Seed round term sheets pending.
- **Assumption:** Co-founder might be relieved to step down if given an honorable exit path.
- **Decision:** Address equity and role structure before signing any investor term sheet.

**The framework (The 10/10/10 Rule & Pre-Mortem):**
- *10 Minutes:* Extreme discomfort and awkwardness during the conversation.
- *10 Months:* The cap table is clean, you own appropriate equity, and your investors trust your governance.
- *10 Years:* The company succeeded because you had the fortitude to make hard decisions early.
- *Pre-Mortem:* The fastest way this startup dies is raising $2M with dead weight owning half your company.

**My take:**
Have the conversation this week, before opening investor due diligence. Offer him an honorable, transparent choice:
1. **Option A (All-In):** Match full-time commitment starting Monday, with clear quarterly OKRs.
2. **Option B (Graceful Transition to Advisor):** Step down from executive operations, retain vested equity earned to date (pro-rated for 12 months), forfeit unvested equity, and transition to a passive advisory role with 2–5% equity.

**The primary risk & mitigation:**
The risk is that he reacts defensively and threatens legal blockage. Mitigate this by reviewing your existing founder agreement and vesting schedule with a startup attorney *before* the dinner, so you know your exact legal baseline.

**GTD Action Bridge (Next Moves):**
1. **[Today]:** Spend 30 minutes with your company corporate attorney to confirm your vesting agreement terms and IP assignment status.
2. **[Tomorrow]:** Invite your co-founder to an in-person dinner on neutral ground (not the office): *"Let's grab dinner Thursday night to align on our roles and long-term vision before we sign Seed terms."*
3. **[Thursday Dinner]:** State the reality calmly: *"I love what we started, but 50/50 requires equal operational commitment. Here are the two clean paths forward."*

---

## Step 3: Closing Ownership Transfer

"Hard conversations are the price of running a real company. Are you ready to book that dinner?"
