# GTD Domain Pack: Marketing
## Selection signals
Campaigns, positioning, brand, paid media, acquisition, retention, content, CRM, funnels, offers, audience research, measurement, channel planning, experiments
## Domain vocabulary
Keep distinct: audience, market, objective, behavior, trigger, proposition, offer, message, channel, creative, conversion, budget, measurement, incrementality, experiment
## Diagnostic questions
Ask only when blocking: What business outcome matters? Which audience behavior should change? What existing behavior are we competing with? What is offered? Where will the message appear? What is the decision window? How will we know activity caused the result?
## Extra brief fields
`business_objective`, `audience`, `behavior_change`, `proposition`, `offer`, `channels`, `budget`, `measurement`, `experiment_design`, `brand_constraints`
## Readiness additions
- objective ties to a measurable business or audience outcome
- audience and desired behavior are defined
- proposition, offer, and channel role are clear enough to execute
- measurement is defined before launch when it matters
## Workstream patterns
- strategy: evidence -> audience/behavior -> proposition -> channel role -> measurement
- campaign: brief -> creative territories -> production -> launch -> measurement -> learning
- paid media: tracking audit -> diagnosis -> hypothesis -> experiment -> readout -> decision
## Review additions
Check audience truth, offer-message fit, channel-context fit, measurement validity, confounders, brand consistency, and whether the plan changes behavior rather than only producing activity
## Completion checks
Assets or plans exist, channel requirements are satisfied, tracking assumptions are checked, success criteria have a time window, and the next decision rule is explicit
## Common traps
Treating reach or clicks as the outcome, inventing audience insight, mixing strategy with a channel checklist, claiming causality from correlation, and adding channels without a defined role
